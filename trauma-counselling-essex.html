Trauma Counselling in Essex

Trauma can affect how you think, feel, relate to others and experience everyday life. I offer a safe, compassionate and professional space to help adults, young people and families understand the impact of trauma and move towards recovery at their own pace.

Understanding

What is trauma counselling?

Trauma counselling helps people understand and recover from the emotional, psychological and physical effects of distressing or overwhelming experiences. Rather than focusing only on what happened, it explores how those experiences continue to affect your thoughts, emotions, relationships, confidence and daily life.

Together we work at a pace that feels manageable, building safety first and only moving towards difficult experiences when you are ready.

Do you recognise any of these experiences?

  • Feeling constantly on edge
  • Panic attacks, nightmares or flashbacks
  • Emotional numbness or feeling disconnected
  • Avoiding certain places, people or memories
  • Difficulty trusting others
  • Anger that feels hard to control
  • Low self-worth, guilt or shame
  • Feeling stuck despite trying to move forward

Different experiences

Trauma can affect people in different ways

There is no single kind of trauma, and no right way to react. What matters is the effect an experience has had on you. Trauma can come from a single event or from experiences that continued over time.

Many people do not realise their present struggles are linked to what they have been through.

  • Childhood neglect, emotional or physical abuse
  • Sexual abuse
  • Domestic abuse and repeated relationship trauma
  • Bullying
  • Bereavement and pregnancy loss
  • Serious accidents and medical trauma
  • Workplace trauma and witnessing violence
  • Addiction in the family
  • Growing up with emotionally unavailable parents

Mind and body

How trauma affects the brain and body

Trauma responses are not a weakness. They are your mind and body trying to keep you safe.

Survival responses

Fight, flight, freeze and fawn are automatic ways of coping with threat. They can stay switched on long after the danger has passed.

An alert nervous system

You may feel constantly on guard, jump at small things or struggle to relax, sleep or feel safe.

Triggers and memory

Reminders can bring feelings flooding back. Trauma is held in the body and emotions as well as in thoughts.

Attachment and trust

Early experiences shape how safe it feels to rely on others, and can echo in adult relationships.

Emotional regulation

Big feelings can feel overwhelming or disappear into numbness. Learning to steady them is part of recovery.

Shame and self-worth

Many people blame themselves. Counselling helps you see what happened, and what was not your fault, more clearly.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I have experienced trauma?

Trauma is less about the event and more about its lasting effect on you. If past experiences still affect your sleep, mood, relationships or sense of safety, it may be worth talking it through.

Do I have to talk about everything?

No. You set the pace. We build trust and safety first, and you decide what you share and when.

How long does trauma counselling take?

It varies from person to person. We review progress together, so you always know where we are.

Can childhood trauma affect adult relationships?

Yes. Early experiences often shape how we trust, attach and cope as adults, and counselling can help you understand and change those patterns.

Can trauma counselling help with anxiety?

Yes. Anxiety is often linked to trauma, and working with the cause as well as the symptoms can bring real relief.

Can counselling help after pregnancy loss?

Yes. Pregnancy loss can be a deep and often unspoken grief. Counselling offers a gentle space to process it.

What if I do not remember everything that happened?

That is common and it is okay. You do not need a complete memory for counselling to help.
