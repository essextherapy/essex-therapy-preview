Understanding

What is addiction?

Addiction is a pattern of using a substance or behaviour that becomes difficult to control, despite the harm it causes. It can involve alcohol, drugs, gambling, prescription medication, gaming, food, work and more.

Often it began as a way of coping with stress, pain, loneliness or past experiences. Counselling helps you understand that story and develop healthier ways of managing your emotions.

Signs you may need support

  • You have tried to cut down or stop and found it hard
  • You hide or play down how much you use
  • It is affecting your relationships, work or health
  • You use to cope with stress, anxiety or low mood
  • Shame or guilt follows each time
  • A loved one's use is affecting you

What addiction can look like

Addiction is more than the substance

Addiction can involve a substance or a behaviour. Underneath it there is often trauma, shame, difficult emotions, mental health struggles or a need to cope. Understanding that is as important as changing the behaviour.

You do not have to have hit rock bottom to ask for help. Many people come because they feel something is slipping out of their control.

  • Alcohol
  • Drugs
  • Gambling
  • Prescription medication
  • Pornography
  • Gaming
  • Shopping or food
  • Social media or work

The cycle

Understanding the cycle of addiction

Seeing the pattern clearly is often the first step to changing it.

Trigger

A feeling, situation, memory or person sets things off.

Emotion

Stress, loneliness, anger or pain becomes hard to bear.

Craving

The urge to escape or numb the feeling builds.

Use

The substance or behaviour provides a way out.

Relief

A brief sense of calm or escape follows.

Shame

Guilt or shame returns, and the cycle begins again.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can counselling help before I stop drinking or using?

Yes. You do not need to be abstinent to start. Counselling can help you understand your use and decide what you want to change.

Do I need rehab?

Not everyone does. We can talk through what level of support suits you and where to get it if you need more.

Can counselling help if I have relapsed?

Yes. A relapse is not a failure. It is information we can learn from together.

Can I come if it is someone else's addiction affecting me?

Absolutely. Many people come to find boundaries, support and perspective.

Can counselling help if I have relapsed?

Yes. A relapse is not a failure. It is information we can learn from together.

How does addiction affect the family?

It can bring fear, conflict, secrecy and role changes for everyone. Family members can get support in their own right.
