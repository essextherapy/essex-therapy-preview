Understanding

What is anxiety counselling?

Anxiety counselling helps you understand why your mind and body react the way they do and develop practical ways to feel steadier. Anxiety can show up physically, emotionally, in your thinking and in your behaviour.

For neurodivergent people, anxiety often comes with sensory overload, masking, executive-function strain and burnout. Therapy can be shaped around how your mind actually works.

You may be struggling with

  • Constant worry or racing thoughts
  • Panic attacks
  • Health, social or performance anxiety
  • Emotional overwhelm and burnout
  • Masking or exhaustion from fitting in
  • Sensory overload
  • Difficulty with boundaries and self-esteem
  • Anxiety linked to past trauma

Understanding anxiety

Types of anxiety

Anxiety is a normal human response, but it can become overwhelming when it is constant or out of proportion. It can look different in different people.

Anxiety shows up in the body, in our thoughts, in our feelings and in what we do or avoid.

  • Generalised anxiety and constant worry
  • Panic attacks and panic disorder
  • Health anxiety
  • Social anxiety
  • Obsessive thoughts and OCD
  • Phobias
  • High-functioning anxiety
  • Anxiety linked to trauma

Neurodiversity

Anxiety and neurodiversity

Many neurodivergent people live with anxiety that is shaped by how they experience the world. Therapy can be adapted to fit how your mind works.

ADHD

Overwhelm, emotional intensity and the strain of keeping up can feed anxiety and burnout.

Autism

Social demands, change and uncertainty can be exhausting and anxiety-provoking.

Sensory overload

Noise, light and crowded places can push your nervous system past its limit.

Masking

Hiding who you are to fit in takes a heavy toll over time.

Executive functioning

Planning and organising difficulties can create constant pressure.

Rejection sensitivity

Fear of criticism or rejection can be intense and painful.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can anxiety go away?

Many people find their anxiety becomes much more manageable. We focus on understanding it and building skills so it no longer runs your life.

Can autism or ADHD cause anxiety?

They can contribute. Living in a world not designed for your brain is stressful, and counselling can support you with that.

Can counselling help with masking?

Yes. We can explore the cost of masking and how to be more yourself safely.

Do you work with young people?

Yes, with adults and young people. Please get in touch to talk about what would help.

Can ADHD cause panic attacks?

ADHD does not directly cause panic attacks, but ongoing overwhelm and stress can make panic more likely. Support can help.

Do you help with depression and low mood?

Yes. Depression often goes hand in hand with anxiety, and counselling can help you understand and ease it.
